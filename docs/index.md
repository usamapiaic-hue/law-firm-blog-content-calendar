# Law Firm Blog Content Calendar

A 12-month blog plan for a small law firm, built around the questions clients actually ask, at a pace a busy firm can keep: two posts a month. Published by Legal Web Agency.

Most law firm blogs stall for one of two reasons: nobody knows what to write about, or the posts answer questions nobody searches for. The fix is to start from intake calls and plan the year in advance, so each month only needs writing, not deciding.

## Step 1: Collect 24 questions

Spend one week writing down every question callers and new clients ask. Group them by practice. You need 24, two per month. Good sources:

| Source | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Intake calls | "Do I have to talk to the insurance company?" |
| First consultations | "How long will my divorce take?" |
| Search Console | Queries your site already appears for |
| Reviews | What clients say they were worried about |

## Step 2: Map them to the year

Some questions are seasonal. Put them in the month people search for them.

| Month | Post 1 (evergreen) | Post 2 (seasonal or timely) |
|-------|--------------------|-----------------------------|
| January | What to do in the first 48 hours after an arrest / accident / separation | New laws that took effect this year in your state |
| February | How fees work at our firm | Tax season and divorce or estate questions |
| March | Deadlines for your main case type | Spring travel and accident risks |
| April | What to bring to a first consultation | Estate planning for new parents |
| May | Myths about your practice area | Summer holidays and custody schedules |
| June | How long cases take, step by step | Boating, pool or road-trip accidents |
| July | What insurance companies will try | Mid-year law changes |
| August | When you do not need a lawyer (honestly) | Back-to-school custody and school-choice questions |
| September | Questions to ask any lawyer before hiring | Workplace and employment questions after summer |
| October | A real process walkthrough, start to finish | Halloween and DUI risks |
| November | Common mistakes clients make | Holiday travel, gifting and estate questions |
| December | Year-end checklist for your practice area | Holiday parties, DUI and liability |

Adjust the seasonal column to your practice; a family law firm and an injury firm will fill it differently.

## Step 3: Write each post the same way

1. Answer the question in the first two or three sentences.
2. Explain the steps or factors in plain language.
3. Add a short FAQ for the follow-up questions.
4. Link to the matching practice page, and end with one clear next step.
5. Have an attorney review it for accuracy and for your state's advertising rules.

## Step 4: Review quarterly

Each quarter, check which posts got impressions and clicks in Search Console. Update the ones on page two, combine posts that compete for the same question, and drop topics nobody searches for from next year's plan.
